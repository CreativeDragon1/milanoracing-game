ENGINEERING
Guide paths use periodic B-spline lateral offsets with track-bound constraints,
a minimum-curvature seed and numerical lap-time minimization. Speed envelopes
use the same mass, tyre grip, drag, power, steering and axle brake-force limits
as the browser model. Forward acceleration and backward braking passes are
iterated around the closed loop. Suggested driving speeds have a 10% reserve.
This is local optimization of a simplified model, not proof of the globally
fastest physical lap. Real F1 vehicle calibration and suspension are not modeled.

MULTIPLAYER
Python standard-library HTTP/HTTPS server. Cryptography generates a fresh
per-host certificate. No shared private key is distributed. Server-owned
timing and checkpoints; client-side vehicle simulation. Casual private-network
ghost racing, not cheat-proof public hosting.

ROAD ELEVATION - 22 OCTOBER 2026
Historical F1 position traces accessed through OpenF1 (2024 qualifying,
three laps per circuit). Source XYZ units documented by the FastF1 project:
https://openf1.org/docs/#location
https://github.com/theOehrly/Fast-F1/blob/master/fastf1/core.py
Coordinates are decimetres. Heights were registered against each mapped layout,
converted to metres, combined by median and spatially smoothed. Relative road
height is preserved; absolute altitude is shifted for rendering. Source URLs,
lap registration errors and processing results are in Elevation Validation.json.
Road, kerbs, runoffs, walls, guide arrows, car pitch, cameras and opponent cars
follow these heights. Gravity on grades and crest/compression tyre loads affect
acceleration, braking and cyclic racing-line speed targets. Lateral guide paths
retain the earlier local optimization; they are not a new full 3D optimum.
Surrounding terrain is inferred from road heights, not a surveyed terrain model.
Banking, crossfall, individual bumps, suspension travel and airborne dynamics
are not reproduced. The original fictional Milano circuit remains flat.

V10 INTEGRATION
Wheel-speed/gear-coupled revs, clutch launch, throttle-dependent exhaust body,
intake bite, ignition-cut upshifts, downshift blips and restrained lift-off pops.
Wind, tyre-slip and kerb sound follow the car state. Camera-dependent filtering,
equal-power RPM blending and compression keep the mix coherent. The recording
is real; source RPM and the simulated drivetrain are approximations.
