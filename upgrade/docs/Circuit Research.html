What the measurements mean

Real circuits use geographic centreline data and terrain registered to that same coordinate system. The TUM FTM database supplies satellite-derived left/right boundaries for nine venues. Its sampled data is historical and varies in accuracy. Registration moves it onto the game's centreline; a smooth profile drives the asphalt, edge lines, kerbs, runoff, barriers, track-limit checks and racing-line constraints.

Monaco and Singapore do not have boundaries in that source. Their widths are explicitly reconstructed estimates. Changed sections at Abu Dhabi and parts of historical Zandvoort also retain estimates. This package does not claim surveyed width accuracy at every section. Each station carries provenance in world-tour-data.json, and registration statistics are in qa/track-widths.json. Milano is an original design, so its 16 m width is intentional.

Terrain uses Mapzen elevation tiles. It is resampled and blended into the independently constructed circuit elevation; it is not a laser scan of the track. Buildings, water, forests and pit routes use OpenStreetMap. Footprints are geographic; missing heights, windows, roof construction and balconies are estimated. Road-overlap conflicts are clipped and recorded.

Background colour uses the commercially reusable 2016/2017 edition of EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless, with mapped land-cover overlays. The images are not current high-resolution orthophotos. The scene uses actual geometry for terrain, barriers, raised kerbs, recessed facades, roofs, balconies, landmark structures and nearby trees. Distant vegetation uses simpler geometry.

Racing line

A bounded periodic spline is optimized for local lap time. Speed is solved using aero load, axle friction circles, steering limits, braking/drive force, gravity, grade and banking. The runtime uses a convergent bracketed speed solver, with braking propagated backwards and acceleration forwards. Driver cues use the speed-squared change over distance and actual current speed. This is a local numerical solution, not a claim of a globally optimal real F1 line.

Typography

Saira was selected for its actual italic and variable-width forms: condensed heavy headings, readable smaller menu text, and clear differentiation from the official F1 typeface. Oxanium supplies timing and telemetry numerals. Racing Sans One was researched as an alternative but was not selected. The authentic Milano logo remains artwork.

Sponsors

All six supplied marks remain intact. Placement uses pit-building fascia, circuit bridges/gantries, barrier wraps and run-off panels, informed by venue maps and race-view conventions. These are Milano-sponsored reconstructions; they do not claim the sponsors occupy those locations at the real Grand Prix.
