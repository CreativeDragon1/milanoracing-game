Qualifying. Strategy. Race.

QUALIFYING

Short: five minutes. Full: Q1 for 18 minutes, Q2 for 15, Q3 for 13. The field goes from 22 to 16 to 10 cars. The valid fastest lap sets each stage's order; equal times use the earlier completion. A lap started before the flag can be finished.

RACE

Qualifying sets your grid slot. A direct race starts you at the back. Five red lights and a varying hold precede the start. Choose 5, 10, 25 laps, or the first lap count exceeding 305 km (260 km at Monaco).

BOX THIS LAP

P / RB calls or cancels the stop. T / LB selects the next compound. Follow the circuit to pit entry; the game assists lane driving, limiting, stopping and release. Service takes 2.5–2.94 seconds plus any unserved time penalty.

Tyre strategy and penalties

Soft, medium and hard compounds have distinct initial grip and wear rates. Wear is scaled to race length, so a five-lap event still has a meaningful stint. The selected two-compound rule requires two different dry compounds: finishing without them gives DSQ. Choosing the same tyre again does not fulfil the rule. Four distinct track-limit excursions incur five seconds; resets also incur five seconds. Qualifying excursions delete the current lap. A time penalty is served during the stop or added to the result.

Race classification uses completed laps, then elapsed time including penalties. Cars under 90% of the winner's completed laps are not classified. Points follow 25–18–15–12–10–8–6–4–2–1. Qualifying's out-lap is not a timed result. Pit timing passes the start line without awarding an extra lap.

Rules reference and game scope

Reference: FIA 2026 Sporting Regulations, issue 06, sections B1.6, B2.4, B2.5 and B6.3. Also F1's weekend guide. These timings are for standard qualifying, not sprint qualifying.

This is a dry, standalone event model for the custom V10 MR 26. It does not implement the complete FIA rulebook: formation laps, Safety Car/VSC, red flags, weather, finite tyre allocations, fuel strategy, parc ferme, the 107% entry rule and steward adjudication are not simulated. Qualifying breaks advance by button rather than making event players wait seven minutes. Pit-lane paths come from maps; exact limiter-loop locations, service marks, widths and gradients are reconstructed. The pit limiter covers the assisted route. Monaco uses 60 km/h; the other venues use 80 km/h. FIA Dutch pit-limit decision. This is not an official Formula 1 product or a regulated championship simulator.

Camera controls

C / Y / Triangle cycles close chase, nose, wide chase and aerial views. Chase looks ahead into the corner and adapts to speed. Pit cameras follow the lane separately so the main-track barrier does not pull the view through the fence.

Use the kerbs

Brief run-offs have a recovery window. Sustained or deep cuts delete timed laps. In races, the first three excursions earn warnings; the fourth adds five seconds. One trip off track counts once. Online and solo races use the same forgiving policy. Reset penalties and checkpoint checks still apply.
